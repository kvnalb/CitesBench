1. CORE EVALUATION PHILOSOPHY
ICLR highly values:
- Fundamental insights into learning representations, not just engineering mashups.
- Mathematical rigor (theory must directly connect to the practical algorithm; "math dressing" is penalized).
- Empirical rigor (strong, contemporary baselines; comprehensive ablations; clear statistical significance).
- Paradigm-shifting ideas over trivial architectural concatenations.

2. CALIBRATION & ANTI-BIAS WARNINGS
- BEWARE THE 'CATCHY PREMISE' TRAP: Do not over-rate papers just because they have an appealing, accessible, or trendy premise (e.g., 'training on 1 GPU', 'democratizing X'). High scores (3.5+) require proof of deep, rigorous execution and comprehensive ablations, not just a cool or well-written idea. Flashy abstracts often mask weak execution.
- AVOID TOP-END COMPRESSION: Differentiate strongly between a 3.0, 4.0, and 5.0. A 3.0-3.5 is a standard, solid accept. A 4.0+ requires undeniable, exhaustive execution and clear superiority. A 4.5+ is exceptionally rare.
- DECOUPLE CONFIDENCE FROM READABILITY: Do not assign a high Confidence just because the text is easy to understand. Confidence reflects your niche-specific technical expertise. Default to 2.5 - 3.5. Reserve 4.0+ ONLY if you can thoroughly verify the mathematical proofs and code-level implementation details.

**Confidence (0.0 - 5.0)**
